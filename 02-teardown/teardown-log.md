# Teardown log: my walkthrough of Revolut investing

I walked the journey on my own Revolut account on Sun Oct 18, 2026, from 10:21 to 10:44 (Standard plan, Revolut Securities Europe UAB, Netherlands), and added the S&P 500 search on Oct 19 after the interviews. 45 screens are logged below; the 13 the PRD relies on are in [screenshots](screenshots/).

These are **observations only**, made before the interviews. Each points to a hypothesis (H1 fees, H2 choice and discoverability, H3 free-trade allowance, H4 onboarding effort; "guardrail" marks a regulatory or harm-avoidance angle). The interviews decided what they mean: see the [synthesis](../03-research/synthesis.md). S08 and S09 have no screenshot. My own answers to the income, net worth and passport questions were deliberately not recorded.

## What the walkthrough suggested (before the interviews)

It took about 21 minutes (10:21 to 10:42) and about 34 screens to reach a buy confirmation, including a detour through the Robo-Advisor and CFDs. The core path alone was investing questions, an account review, a free-trade banner, the order screen and a face check.

- **H1 (fee surprise): weakened.** The confirmation screen shows fees before Submit. What remains is wording ("No fees" beside an exchange fee) and the cost of the next trade. Competitors also charge about €1 on small manual orders.
- **H2 (too much choice, or the right product is hard to find): strengthened.** Before buying, a first-timer meets five product types, two goal questionnaires, a "Buy" suggestion with a 62.7 percent expected return, repeated CFD offers, and commission-free index funds three taps deep.
- **H3 (free-trade allowance): partly supported.** The allowance appears once, as a banner on a stock page; the confirmation does not say a free trade was used.
- **H4 (onboarding effort): new candidate.** Two questionnaires, a passport number, a review wait and a face check before the first $10 purchase. Mostly regulation, so only a finding if interviewees said it made them quit.

What the interviews then showed: H1, H3 and H4 did not hold up, and H2 held only in a narrower form (choosing between funds, not finding them). See the [synthesis](../03-research/synthesis.md#4-what-happened-to-each-hypothesis).

## Fee notes (Secondary, checked Oct 18, 2026)

- **Stocks, cost PDF:** Revolut's ex-ante costs disclosure for US-listed stocks, version 7.0 (Revolut Securities Europe UAB), read Oct 18, 2026 at `cdn.revolut.com/legal/terms/RSEUAB/ex-ante-costs-report-US-stocks-v7.0-EN.pdf`: no free trades on Standard and Plus, a fixed €1.00 per trade outside the allowance, 5 free trades on Premium, 10 on Metal and Ultra; US stocks trade in USD with an extra currency fee above a €1,000 monthly exchange limit on Standard (1 percent) and Plus (0.5 percent). **Update:** the app itself says 1 free trade a month on Standard (S26), so this PDF is probably not the version that applies. The in-app figure wins. A third-party review (EEA-focused) said 1 free trade then the greater of 0.25 percent or €1; another site said 3 free orders then $1. Fees differ by region, entity and date.
- **Robo-Advisor:** Revolut help centre lists an annual management fee of 0.75 percent on every plan, charged monthly on the average daily balance, plus the ETFs' own expense ratios and, depending on country, VAT on the management fee. A third-party review (Curvo, May 2026) said the robo starts from €100; the app says €10 (S22, S24), so the app wins.
- **Still open:** what the second trade in a month costs, shown in-app.

## Public sources found (Secondary, checked Oct 18, 2026)

- Revolut stocks page (`revolut.com/stocks/`): from €1, 4,000+ stocks, commission-free within plan allowance.
- Revolut "Description of Services, Financial Instruments and Risks" (EEA entity): for simple products like stocks there is no appropriateness assessment; a questionnaire is needed for complex products and a suitability questionnaire for the Robo-Advisor.
- Revolut help centre, UK crypto onboarding: a knowledge quiz, limited attempts and a 24-hour wait. A contrast: designed friction in one asset class, none in another.
- Revolut 2025 annual results (March 2026): 68.3 million retail customers, Wealth revenue up 31 percent to $876m, over 63 percent of new retail customers joined by word of mouth or referral, zero-commission ETF investment plans rolled out in the EEA and Switzerland. The referral figure matters: first-timers often arrive through friends.
- Upvest case study: Revolut used Upvest's investment API to add ETFs and fractional European stocks across the EEA about 6 months after teaming up, and launched a robo-advisor in February 2024.
- FCA research "Gaming trading": an experiment with over 9,000 consumers found push notifications and prize draws can increase trading frequency and risk-taking. FCA discussion paper (Dec 2025) notes apps with five or more engagement features showed significantly worse returns, though other factors may be involved. (UK regulator; Revolut's EEA entity is regulated in Lithuania, so use as guardrail context only.)
- I found no published interviews with Revolut investing users, so my interviews fill a real gap. App-store and review-site sentiment exists but is self-selected, and I did not use it as evidence.
