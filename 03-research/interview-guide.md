# Interview guide

The guide I used for all six interviews: about 30 minutes, past behaviour first, screens last. Segments: 2 never invested, 2 started recently, 2 invest regularly. Participants are recorded by number and segment only.

## Recruiting

I messaged 8 to 10 students on Oct 18 and ran six interviews on Oct 19. The message:

> "Hey! I'm doing a short research project on how people get started with investing apps. Would you be up for a 20-minute chat this week? Whether you invest or have never tried, your experience is useful. No prep needed, I'm just asking what happened the last time you looked into it. I'll keep your name out of anything I write. Free for a call or a coffee on campus on Monday or Tuesday?"

## Before each interview

- Ask permission to take notes, and to record audio. Say that names stay out of the project.
- Open a fresh [note sheet](interview-note-template.md) with a participant number and segment only.
- Have my phone ready on the Invest tab with name and balance hidden, for people who have never invested.
- Never mention the hypotheses, and never say anything is wrong with the app.

## The 30 minutes

| Part | Minutes | Goal | What I ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Warm-up | 2 | Put them at ease | "I'm researching how people start investing. There are no right answers. I just want to hear what really happened." |
| 2. Their story | 8 | How they got to investing, or why not | "Tell me about the first time you thought about investing. What did you do next? Who or what influenced you? What slowed you down? What was signing up like?" |
| 3. The last time | 8 | Real behaviour, step by step | "Walk me through the last time you bought, or tried to buy, something. What did you check first? How did you choose? Did anything surprise you? What did the app suggest, and where was it on screen?" |
| 4. Costs and trust | 4 | What they know about costs | "When did you first learn what it costs? What did you expect to pay? What did you actually pay? Which plan are you on, and how many free trades do you get?" |
| 5. Show me | 6 | Watch, do not ask | Screen task below |
| 6. Close | 2 | Referrals and gaps | "Is there anyone else I should talk to? What should I have asked that I didn't?" |

For people who have never invested, part 3 becomes "Tell me about the last time you looked into it, even if you gave up", and part 4 becomes "What do you think it would cost, and where does that idea come from?"

### Screen task (always after parts 2 to 4)

- **If they invest:** they open their own app and show how they would find something to hold for ten years. They think out loud, keep their balance hidden and skip anything they prefer not to show. I take no screenshots and write down no amounts.
- **If they have never invested:** they use my phone on the Invest tab. "Imagine you want to put 50 euros into something you'd hold for ten years. Think out loud. What would you tap first?" I stop them before any order.

I note what they tap first, where they hesitate, whether and how fast they find a broad index fund, and anything they misread. If they say "I would...", I ask what they did last time. If they ask what to do, I ask "What would you do if I weren't here?"

*Revision during fieldwork (Oct 19): the first version used a screenshot for the screen task. I switched to watching people in a real app, which gives behaviour instead of opinion. Interviews 1 and 2 did not include the screen task.*

## What I listened for

| Hypothesis | Supports it | Disproves it | Best part |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 Fees surprise people | "I didn't know it cost that"; fee noticed only afterwards | They knew the cost, or it did not matter | 4 |
| H2 Choice or discoverability | Long searching, famous names by default, never finding index funds | They picked quickly and can say why | 3 and screen task |
| H3 Free-trade allowance | Cannot say their plan or allowance | Know their allowance | 4 |
| H4 Onboarding effort | Stopped, delayed or nearly quit at sign-up | Sign-up felt easy | 2 |
| Something new | Any problem raised without prompting | n/a | 6 |

## Decision rule, written before the first interview

A problem counts only if **at least 3 of 6 raise it unprompted**, or **2 describe it as a reason they stopped or lost money**. If nothing passes, the honest conclusion is that the product is strong here, and the PRD names the smallest real gap.

## Rules I kept

- Past behaviour before opinions: behaviour is evidence, opinions are cheap.
- Never pitch a solution or defend the app.
- Screens come last, so they cannot shape what people remember.
- Six people is early evidence, not proof.
- Quotes under 15 words, no names.
