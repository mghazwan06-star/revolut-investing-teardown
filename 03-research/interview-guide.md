# Interview kit

Use for each of the 5 to 6 interviews. About 30 minutes, three parts: past behaviour first, screens second, close last. Segments: 2 never invested, 2 started recently, 2 invest regularly. Participants are recorded by number and segment only.

## Before each interview

- [ ] Pilot the guide once with a friend (about 20 minutes) before the first real interview. It finds awkward questions early.
- [ ] Ask permission to take notes, and to record audio if wanted. Say names stay out of the project.
- [ ] Open the note sheet ([note template](../03-research/interview-note-template.md)). Write only a participant number and segment.
- [ ] Have your phone ready on the Invest tab with name and balance hidden (for people who have never invested), plus the redacted screenshots S01 and S38 in the redacted screenshots as a fallback.
- [ ] Phone on silent. One person asks, one writes, if possible.
- [ ] Do not mention hypotheses, and do not say anything is wrong with the app.

## Interview flow, 30 minutes

| Part | Minutes | Goal | What you ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Warm-up | 2 | Put them at ease | "I'm researching how people start investing. No right answers. I want to hear what really happened." |
| 2 Their story | 8 | How they got to investing, or why not | "Tell me about the first time you thought about investing. What did you do next? Who or what influenced you? What slowed you down?" |
| 3 The last time | 8 | Real behaviour, step by step | "Walk me through the last time you bought, or tried to buy, something. What did you check? How did you choose? What did it cost? Anything surprise you?" |
| 4 Costs and trust | 4 | Fee awareness | "When did you first learn what it costs? What did you expect? What did you actually pay?" |
| 5 Show me | 6 | Watch, do not ask | Screen task below |
| 6 Close | 2 | Referrals and gaps | "Anyone else I should talk to? What should I have asked?" |

### Screen task (only after parts 2 to 4)

Updated Oct 19: instead of only a screenshot, watch real behaviour. Pick the version that fits the person. Always think out loud, always stay quiet while they work, and never let anyone reach a Submit or Buy button.

**A. They invest (started recently or regularly).** Ask them to open their own app and show you how they would make their next purchase, or how they first found their main fund. "Think out loud. What would you tap first?" Tell them to keep their balance hidden or to skip any screen they do not want to show. Do not take screenshots or photos of their screen, and write down no amounts. If they use Trade Republic or DEGIRO, also ask: "Show me how you first found your index fund." This tells us where those products sit in their apps, which text sources cannot.

**B. They have never invested.** Hand them your phone on the Invest tab (or share your screen) with your balance and name hidden. Say: "Imagine you want to put 50 euros into something you would hold for ten years. Think out loud. What would you tap first?" Stop them before any order is placed. Fallback if you cannot hand over a phone: the cropped Invest tab screenshot (S01) and, if needed, the ETF list (S38) from the redacted screenshots.

**What to note (both versions):** what they tap first and how long they hesitate; whether they find a broad index fund and how long it takes; anything they say they do not understand; anything they skip or misread. If they say "I would...", ask what they did the last time. If they ask what to do, answer "What would you do if I were not here?"

**Time:** about 6 minutes. If the call runs long, shorten part 2 or 3, not this.

### Probes that work

"What happened next?" "How did that feel?" "Can you show me?" "Why was that?" "Tell me about the last time." Count to five in your head before the next question.

## What we listen for

| Hypothesis | Supports it if they say | Disproves it if they say | Best question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 Fees surprise people | "I didn't know it cost that"; fee noticed only afterwards | They knew the cost beforehand and it did not matter | Part 4 |
| H2 Choice or discoverability | Long searching, defaulting to famous names, never finding index funds | They picked quickly and can say why | Part 3 and screen task |
| H3 Free-trade allowance | They cannot say which plan they are on or how many free trades they get | They know their allowance | Part 4 |
| H4 Onboarding effort | They stopped, delayed or nearly quit during signup | Signup felt easy and quick | Part 2 |
| Something new | Any problem not expected, raised without prompting | n/a | "What should I have asked?" |

## Picking the problem (Wednesday morning)

1. Put every friction moment from all notes on one board, one note each.
2. Group the ones that mean the same thing.
3. For each group, count how many of the 5 to 6 people raised it unprompted and how bad it was (annoyed, delayed, stopped, lost money).
4. Rank by how many people times how bad.
5. Pick one problem and write it as a job statement: "When ___, I want to ___, so I can ___."
6. Write one line on what would have changed our mind.

**Decision rule, set in advance:** a problem counts only if at least 3 of the 6 raise it unprompted, or 2 describe it as a reason they stopped or lost money. If nothing passes, the honest PRD says the product is strong here and names the smallest real gap. (Muhammad was invited to adjust this rule before the interviews; no change was requested.)

## Rules to keep it honest

- Past behaviour before opinions. Opinions are cheap, behaviour is evidence.
- Never pitch a solution or defend the app.
- Screens come last so they cannot bias what people remember.
- Five people is early evidence, not proof. The PRD says so.
- Short quotes only (under 15 words), no names.
