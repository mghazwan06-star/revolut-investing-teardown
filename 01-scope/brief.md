# Project brief

Written Oct 18, 2026, before any interview. Updated Oct 19 when I narrowed the user to students.

## Scope (decision G1)

- **User:** a student (about 18 to 25) in the Netherlands or wider EEA who is a first-time or early-stage investor. *Originally any first-time investor; narrowed on Oct 19 because all six interviewees were students and a narrower user gives a sharper PRD.*
- **Journey:** from opening the Invest tab to a first purchase the person feels confident about.
- **Question:** where do first-timers lose confidence or get surprised?
- **Why this slice:** one journey studied in depth shows more product thinking than a shallow teardown of the whole app.
- **Product context (checked in-app, Oct 18):** my account is served by Revolut Securities Europe UAB, regulated by the Bank of Lithuania, on the Standard plan.

## Starting hypotheses

Written before the interviews, as guesses to test. Each has a test that could prove it wrong.

| # | Hypothesis | What I would hear if true | Where I test it | What would disprove it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Fees surprise people on small first purchases | "I didn't know it would cost that." Fee noticed only at confirmation | Walkthrough; interview part 4 | People knew the fee beforehand and were not bothered |
| H2 | Too much choice, or the right product being hard to find, stalls the first purchase | Long searching, defaulting to famous names, never finding index funds, giving up | Walkthrough; interview part 3 and screen task | People choose quickly and can say why |
| H3 | The monthly free-trade allowance is confusing | They cannot say which plan they are on or how many free trades they get | Walkthrough; interview part 4 | People know their allowance |
| H4 | Onboarding effort (two questionnaires, passport number, review wait, face check) makes people stall or quit | They stopped, delayed or nearly quit during sign-up | Interview part 2 | Sign-up felt easy and quick |

H4 was added after the walkthrough. The verdict on each is in the [synthesis](../03-research/synthesis.md#4-what-happened-to-each-hypothesis).

## Non-goals, on purpose

- A teardown of all of Revolut investing or the whole app
- Detailed screen designs: the proposal stays at principles until a prototype test
- More than three competitors
- A financial model or invented numbers: metric targets are proposals with "baseline to be measured"
- Pitching a solution during interviews
- Names of any interviewee, anywhere
- Claiming statistical proof from six interviews: it is early evidence

## Evidence rules

| Label | What it is | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary** | My app walkthrough and my six interviews | Choosing the problem and evidencing it |
| **Secondary** | Revolut's own pages and legal documents, annual results, regulator research, competitors' fee pages | Context, cross-checks and guardrails |

Public sources support or challenge the interviews; they never replace them. Every fee and feature is tagged with the date checked, and an in-app figure beats any third-party figure. Sources are listed in [sources.md](../sources.md).
